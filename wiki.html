(Redirected from List of government schemes in India)

The Government of India has social welfare and social security schemes for India's citizens funded either by the central government, state government or concurrently.[1] Schemes that the central government fully funds are referred to as "central sector schemes" (CS). In contrast, schemes mainly funded by the center and implemented by the states are "centrally sponsored schemes" (CSS).[2] In the 2022 Union budget of India, there are 740 central sector (CS) schemes.[3][4] and 65 (+/-7) centrally sponsored schemes (CSS).[5]

From 131 CSSs in February 2021, the union government aimed to restructure/revamp/rationalize these by the next year.[6] In 2022 CSS's numbered 65 with a combined funding of ₹442,781 crore (equivalent to ₹5.0 trillion or US$52 billion in 2023).[7] In 2022, there were 157 CSs and CSSs with individual funding of over ₹500 crore (equivalent to ₹561 crore or US$58 million in 2023) each.[8] Central sector scheme actual spending in 2017-18 was ₹587,785 crore (equivalent to ₹6.6 trillion or US$68 billion in 2023),[9] in 2019-20 it was ₹757,091 crore (equivalent to ₹8.5 trillion or US$88 billion in 2023) while the budgeted amount for 2021-22 is ₹1,051,703 crore (equivalent to ₹12 trillion or US$120 billion in 2023).[10] Schemes can also be categorised as flagship schemes.[11] 10 flagship schemes were allocated ₹1.5 lakh crore (equivalent to ₹1.7 trillion or US$17 billion in 2023) in the 2021 Union budget of India.[11] The subsidy for kerosene, started in the 1950s, was slowly decreased since 2009 and eliminated in 2022.[12][13][14]

Implementation of government schemes varies between schemes, and locations, and depends on factors such as evaluation process, awareness, accessibility, acceptability, and capability for last-mile implementation.[15] Government bodies undertaking evaluations and audits include NITI Aayog,[16] Ministry of Statistics and Programme Implementation,[17] and the Comptroller and Auditor General of India.[18]

Evaluations

[edit]

The Development Monitoring and Evaluation Office (DMEO) under the NITI Aayog is responsible for evaluations.[16] Evaluation problems exist.[16] Ministries such as the transport ministry can show immediate physical outcomes of their schemes, whereas in health-related schemes, in certain cases, there is no output to show.[16] Surveys trying to ascertain whether someone has benefitted from a scheme or not can result in respondents denying having benefitted with the hope of receiving the benefit again.[17] This kind of respondent bias in its various forms is being addressed by the Ministry of Statistics and Programme Implementation.[17] The Comptroller and Auditor General of India also assesses the implementation of these schemes.[18]

Effectiveness

[edit]

A number of schemes of successive governments are effective, while others are not effective.[159][160][161][162][163] Despite various schemes and programmes targeted towards hunger, nutrition remains a severe challenge.[164] The Supreme Court has advised the government to keep finances in mind when coming out with schemes.[165] An example given was the Right of Children to Free and Compulsory Education Act, 2009, where there is a shortage of both schools and teachers.[165] Since their inception, flagship welfare schemes of the Modi government, such as Namami Gange and Ayushman Bharat, have been sanctioned more than what has been spent.[166] A key issue is identifying who should receive scheme benefits and how.[167] In 2017, the Comptroller and Auditor General of India (CAG) called Beti Bachao Beti Padhao a failure as per its own objectives.[168]

Awareness

[edit]

The government runs various initiatives to increase awareness of government schemes.[169][170][171][172] Awareness of schemes affects the implementation of the schemes as well as who beneficiaries vote for and who they hold responsible for the success or failure of a scheme - the state or center.[173][174] In 2021-2022 the Government of Goa launched a scheme Swayampurna Goa which involves a government official making eligible beneficiaries aware of the schemes and how to avail them.[175][176] Nearly 80% of Beti Bachao Beti Padhao funds during 2016-2019 was spent on media advocacy.[177]

Political credit and blame

[edit]

There is no official or legalised credit-sharing mechanism between the center and states for the schemes.[174]

In 2014, Congress blamed the Modi government for taking credit for schemes undertaken by previous governments.[178][179] In 2017, Bharatiya Janata Party (BJP) blamed Telangana Rashtra Samithi (TRS) for taking credit.[180] The Himachal Pradesh government blamed NITI Aayog for a cut in funding to centrally sponsored schemes.[181] In 2019, Mamata Banerjee blamed Modi for taking credit for Ayushman Bharat.[182] In 2020, Modi blamed Aam Aadmi Party for not implementing a centrally sponsored sector scheme.[183] In 2021, Smriti Irani blamed Mamata Banerjee for taking credit for schemes of the central government.[184] BJP blamed Jharkhand Mukti Morcha for not implementing schemes properly.[185]

Welfare schemes are used as electoral campaigns, with beneficiaries treated as voters.[186] Bharatiya Janata Party (BJP) has used its implementation of schemes as a vote bank in the 2017 and 2022 Uttar Pradesh Legislative Assembly elections, and the 2019 Indian general election.[186]

Rationalisation

[edit]

Rationalisation is a means to improve governance of schemes.[187] In 2002 (Vajpayee govt) there were about 360 CSSs.[188] In 2022(Modi govt) there were 704 CSs.[4] In 2016 a committee came out with the recommendation of rationalising, revamping and restructuring 66 CSSs, grouping them into umbrella schemes, core schemes and optional schemes on the basis of which identification would be easier and funding patterns would differ, among other recommendations.[189][190]

In the case of a mission, or a scheme or programme becoming a mission or being coupled under a mission, it can mean a signal of prioritization with regard to implementation and funding.[19] Similar schemes can be merged into each other and revamped.[7] A mission can be merged into a new scheme.
